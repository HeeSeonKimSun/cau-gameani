Undergraduate program

The undergraduate program of the Department of Game Contents & Animation is a four-year (eight-semester) Bachelor of Arts (Intensive Course) degree program. Students move from foundation courses in Year 1 to exploration in Year 2 and advanced study in Year 3, culminating in a graduation project in Year 4. Major Foundation, Major Required and Major Elective courses are arranged by year so that students complete them step by step. Each semester, students build a body of work through project-based studios and coursework exhibitions, while joint production projects with art schools abroad and special lectures by industry professionals complement the regular curriculum.

Curriculum by Year

Year 1

Foundations

Foundations

Students complete the shared School of Global Arts curriculum, centered on foundation courses such as ideation and expression, directing and storytelling.

Year 2

Explorations in Digital Arts

Explorations in Digital Arts

Regular major courses begin. Students learn 2D/3D graphics, animation and rigging, cinematic directing, game graphics, and the understanding and implementation of game engines step by step.

Year 3

Advanced Techniques

Advanced Techniques

In project-based courses such as Previsualization, Affective Game Computing and Prototype, students create works in teams or individually.

Year 4

Capstone

Capstone

Continuing their project courses, students complete their graduation projects and present them in the Graduation Exhibition and their portfolios.

Extracurricular Programs

Extracurricular programs are department programs run outside the regular curriculum. They aim to broaden students' experience of international exchange and teamwork. The International Exchange Development Project, special lectures and similar activities are registered and run as university extracurricular programs (Rainbow System).

International Exchange Development ProjectStudents from art schools in several countries form teams and create a work together over one semester, experiencing different production methods and the process of international collaboration firsthand.
Special lecturesEach semester, alongside the semester opening assembly, the Department invites speakers from the game and animation industries and from universities abroad to share their production experience and talk about careers.
GAME&ANI JAMA two- to three-day team production event organized by the Student Council. In a short time, teams complete a game or animation from planning to presentation.
Coursework Exhibitions · ExhibitionsStudents present their work to audiences at coursework exhibitions that gather each semester's work, the Graduation Exhibition, screenings and exhibitions at arts festivals, and festival booths.
Work Feedback · International WorkshopsFaculty from partner universities visit to review student work in person and give feedback.
Career counselingThrough one-on-one advising with faculty, students plan exchange study, study abroad, graduate school and career paths.
